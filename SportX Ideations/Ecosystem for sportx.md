# 1. The Indian Cricket Ecosystem

India's cricket structure operates across six distinct levels. Understanding each level — its players, structure, data availability, and relationship to the next — is the foundation of SportX's product strategy.

## 1.1 Level Overview

|**Level**|**Category**|**Active Players**|**Governing Body**|**Data Availability**|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|L1|Elite Professional (IPL + National)|~150–200|BCCI / IPL Franchise|Complete — public|
|L2|Domestic Senior (Ranji, SMAT, VHT)|~950|BCCI / State Associations|Complete — public|
|L3|National Pathway Age Groups (U-23, U-19, U-16)|~3,800|BCCI / State Associations|Mostly available|
|L4|District (DCA Competitions)|~350,000|District Cricket Associations|Patchy — improving|
|L5|Academy & Grassroots Clubs|~2–3M serious|Private Academies / Clubs|Fragmented|
|L6|School Level (inter-school, SGFI)|~13M+ total|SGFI / School Boards|Minimal — offline|

## 1.2 School Level (Level 6)

School cricket is the widest base of India's cricket pyramid. It operates through multiple overlapping structures:

•        SGFI (School Games Federation of India): Governs inter-school national championships. Runs U-14, U-17, and U-19 age groups. Requires Aadhaar or 10th class certificate for eligibility. Data is uploaded to an SGFI portal for national selections — but remains largely inaccessible outside the system.

•        CBSE/CISCE Affiliated Tournaments: Run by school boards. Inter-house, inter-school, district, and national levels. Private schools like DPS, Kendriya Vidyalaya, etc. run internal programmes.

•        State Education Department Leagues: Each state education department runs its own school games calendar, sometimes in coordination with state cricket associations.

•        Private Academy-Affiliated Schools: High-end cricket academies partner with schools to run talent identification programmes. These are the most data-rich environments at school level.

Key insight: Most school-level cricket is managed by school principals and PT teachers, not by cricket administrators. There is almost no systematic flow of player data from school level to cricket associations. The SGFI portal captures only national-level selected players — the vast majority never get digitised.

## 1.3 Academy Level (Level 5)

Private cricket academies are the most important talent-development layer in Indian cricket outside the official BCCI pathway. They bridge school cricket and district cricket.

•        India has an estimated 10,000–15,000 registered cricket academies, ranging from elite high-performance centres (SCA, MRF Pace Foundation, Rahul Dravid Cricket Academy) to local coaching grounds.

•        Many elite players — including Bumrah, Hardik Pandya, and Yashasvi Jaiswal — were identified and developed at the academy level before entering official district structures.

•        Academy cricket data is entirely fragmented. Each academy maintains its own paper registers, WhatsApp groups, and Excel sheets. There is no central database.

•        Some large academies use CricHeroes to record match data, but internal training data, batch records, and progression history are not systematically captured anywhere.

•        This is the level where family investment is highest (families spend ₹15,000–₹2,00,000+ per year on coaching), and where the emotional and financial stakes of not being discovered are greatest.

## 1.4 District Level (Level 4) — The Primary SportX Target

This is the most strategically important level for SportX's MVP. District cricket is the first point of official, structured competition in India's cricket pathway.

•        Governed by District Cricket Associations (DCAs), which are registered units under BCCI-affiliated state associations. There are 700+ DCAs across India.

•        Players at this level have formally registered with their DCA, have played in official tournaments, and many already have profiles on CricHeroes.

•        Tournament formats include district leagues, inter-district competitions, and age-group trials (U-14, U-16, U-19, U-23, senior).

•        A player competing at this level has self-selected as serious — they have already navigated the academy system and been recognised enough to play for district teams.

•        The discovery gap is acute here: a player averaging 55 in Bihar or Assam is genuinely invisible to state selectors in cities 400+ km away. No one is watching.

•        CricHeroes penetration is estimated at 40–60% of this population — meaning the data infrastructure exists for at least half of all district players already.

## 1.5 State Level (Level 3 + Level 2)

State cricket is where the official BCCI pathway begins. Players at this level are known to franchises and selectors.

•        Level 3 (National Pathway Age Groups): U-16 Vijay Merchant Trophy, U-19 Cooch Behar Trophy, U-23 CK Nayudu Trophy. Approximately 3,800 active players across 38 state teams.

•        Level 2 (Senior Domestic): Ranji Trophy (first-class), Vijay Hazare Trophy (List A), Syed Mushtaq Ali Trophy (T20). Match fees range from ₹40,000–₹60,000 per day.

•        Data at this level is fully public: scorecards are on Cricinfo, ESPNcricinfo, and BCCI.tv. Players have formal BCCI registration IDs.

•        IPL franchises employ 10–30 scouts who monitor all televised domestic T20 and List A matches. The discovery problem is largely solved at this level — SportX's role here shifts from discovery to analytics.

  

# 2. Where SportX Creates Maximum Impact

## 2.1 The Critical Gap: Level 4 → Level 3

The most significant, most solvable, and most commercially attractive gap in Indian cricket is between district-level players (Level 4) and the national pathway (Level 3). This is SportX's primary opportunity.

|**Dimension**|**Level 5 (Academy/Grassroots)**|**Level 4 (District)**|**Level 2→1 (Domestic→IPL)**|
|---|---|---|---|
|Player seriousness|Mixed — many hobbyists|High — self-selected competitive|Professional|
|Data existence|Fragmented / absent|Patchy but available|Fully public|
|Discovery gap|Too wide to bridge at MVP|Real and unsolved|Largely solved by scouts|
|Verification feasibility|Very hard|Achievable|Trivial|
|Scout/buyer motivation|Low|High (state selectors)|Very high (IPL) but served|
|CricHeroes overlap|Low penetration|40–60% penetration|Full public data|
|Partnership dependency|Very high|Manageable|None needed|
|SportX's role|Post-MVP expansion|Discovery platform — MVP|Analytics tool — V2|

## 2.2 The Level 4 Opportunity in Detail

350,000 serious competitive cricketers who are actively trying to progress — but who remain invisible outside their immediate geography because no unified infrastructure exists to surface them. Key facts:

•        A player in Bihar averaging 55 runs per district innings is statistically elite by any measure. But no state selector in Patna is systematically watching every district game across all 38 districts in the state.

•        State trial processes in most states still run on WhatsApp forwards, personal recommendations, and which academy you trained at — not on merit-indexed databases.

•        The IPL has demonstrated demand: CricHeroes' query to find T20 tournaments in April revealed matches in Nellore, Palakkad, and Hooghly — districts the scouting community has almost zero visibility on.

•        One confirmed IPL scout publicly stated: 'I long for the day when all the games taped by the BCCI are put up on an online server that every franchise can access.' That server doesn't exist at district level. That is SportX.

  

# 3. Bottlenecks at Each Level

## 3.1 School Level Bottlenecks

•        No digital identity: Players have no persistent cricket profile. A century at inter-school level in Class 9 is not documented anywhere that a selector can access in Class 11.

•        Institutional gatekeeping: Only schools with formal PT infrastructure and a culture of cricket (typically urban private schools) participate in SGFI. Rural government schools are structurally excluded.

•        No pathway articulation: Students and parents don't know how to convert school performance into a formal cricket pathway. The bridge between school cricket and DCA registration is entirely informal.

•        Data siloed in SGFI portal: Even where national-level school data exists (SGFI portal), it is not accessible to cricket associations for scouting purposes.

## 3.2 Academy Level Bottlenecks

•        No standardised metrics: Each academy uses different evaluation criteria. A 'Grade A' player at one academy has no equivalency at another.

•        Coach dependency: Player visibility entirely depends on whether their specific coach has connections with district selectors. Talent discovery is a social network problem, not a merit problem.

•        No cross-academy visibility: An outstanding player who moves academies loses all historical performance data. There is no portable performance record.

•        Pay-to-trial culture: Most serious trials require academy affiliation or direct payment (₹2,000–₹10,000 per trial). Players from economically weaker backgrounds are structurally disadvantaged.

•        Fake academy certificates: A significant verification problem — certificates from unknown academies are freely forged and widely used in trial applications.

## 3.3 District Level Bottlenecks

•        Geographic invisibility: State selectors are concentrated in capital cities. District games in interior regions are unwatched. No video, no scorecards in a central location, no ability to surface data to decision-makers.

•        Registration gatekeeping loop: To play in official district matches, a player often needs political backing from club officials or DCA members. Independent players from small towns face structural exclusion before they even get to perform.

•        Inconsistent scorecard quality: Some DCAs use CricHeroes, some use paper registers, some use Excel. Data quality varies enormously across the 700+ DCAs. Some DCAs have no digital records at all.

•        No single source of truth: A player's career statistics across multiple seasons may be spread across CricHeroes, paper scorecards, WhatsApp photos, and the coach's memory.

•        Age fraud: One of the most serious systemic problems. Overage players competing in U-19 or U-23 tournaments — a well-documented issue that erodes the credibility of district cricket entirely.

•        No benchmark: Players have no way to understand how their statistics compare to state-level standards. A player averaging 35 doesn't know if that's good enough for state trials.

## 3.4 State Level Bottlenecks

•        State trial opacity: Selection processes vary enormously across 38 state associations. Some run open trials, some select based on DCA recommendations, some are influenced by political relationships with academy owners.

•        Under-resourced selectors: State selection panels are volunteer-run in many states. They don't have the bandwidth to systematically scout all 700+ DCAs in their state. They rely on trusted intermediaries (coaches, ex-players) — which recreates the network-dependency problem.

•        Women's pathway near-invisible: Despite BCCI's investment in women's cricket, the female talent pipeline at district and academy level is almost entirely undocumented. A significant opportunity SportX should not ignore.

## 4.2 CricHeroes as the Primary L4 Data Backbone

CricHeroes is the single most important external data source for SportX at Level 4. Key facts about the platform:

•        40 million+ registered cricketers on the platform — the largest grassroots cricket database in the world.

•        Revenue of $13.4M in 2023 with a team of 78 people — a genuinely profitable, deeply entrenched platform.

•        Covers tournament scoring, live streaming, player profiles, team management, ground management, and academy listings.

•        Primary competitive risk: CricHeroes has the data AND the distribution but has not built a scout-facing discovery product. Their business model is ad-supported community product, not B2B scouting tool. This is SportX's structural opening.

•        Partnership strategy: SportX should pursue CricHeroes data integration (API or data-sharing agreement) rather than trying to replicate their data collection. The scout-side product is SportX's moat, not the scoring data.

## 4.3 Data Gaps SportX Must Bridge

•        ~40–60% of Level 4 players are not on CricHeroes — paper scorecard OCR pipeline is essential for these players.

•        Video evidence exists informally (WhatsApp, YouTube) but is not linked to verified player identities. A structured video submission system with identity verification is a significant gap.

•        Contextual data (pitch conditions, opposition quality, tournament level) is almost never captured. Without context, a 50-run innings is uninterpretable. SportX should capture this at the match-registration level.

  

# 5. Minimum Eligibility & Verification Criteria

## 5.1 Eligibility Criteria by Level

|**Player Level**|**Minimum Eligibility**|**Required Documents**|
|---|---|---|
|School (L6)|Currently enrolled in recognised school. Age per SGFI norms.|School ID card, Aadhaar / Date of birth certificate, School NOC|
|Academy (L5)|Enrolled in registered academy OR active in club cricket. Min. 10 competitive matches.|Academy enrollment letter OR club registration, CricHeroes profile (preferred), Coach reference|
|District (L4) — Primary MVP Target|Registered with District Cricket Association OR played in official DCA tournament. Min. 5 official matches.|DCA registration OR scorecard from official DCA tournament, Aadhaar for age verification, Coach confirmation (P4 on platform)|
|State Age Group (L3)|Selected for or trialled by state association age-group team.|State association selection letter OR BCCI-registered state squad listing|
|Senior Domestic (L2)|Played in Ranji / SMAT / VHT for state association.|BCCI Player ID (auto-verifiable from public records)|

## 5.2 Verification Tiers

SportX should implement a four-tier trust system rather than a binary verified/unverified flag:

•        Tier 0 (Auto-Verified): BCCI-registered players with confirmed domestic records. Trust granted automatically via ID cross-check. Applies to L2 and L3 players.

•        Tier 1 (Institutional Verification): Academy admin or DCA official has confirmed enrollment and participation. Coach confirmation on platform. CricHeroes sync within 15% tolerance. Target for L4 players within 30 days of signup.

•        Tier 2 (Self-Reported + Partial Verification): Player has uploaded scorecards (OCR processed), provided Aadhaar, and received peer confirmation from 2+ teammates. No academy admin sign-off yet.

•        Tier 3 (Unverified — Scout-Hidden): Account created, no verification completed. Profile visible only to player. Hidden from all scout views to prevent low-trust noise on the discovery side.

## 5.3 Flagging False or Unverified Records

•        Age fraud detection: Cross-reference Aadhaar date of birth against DCA registration age and SGFI records where available. Any discrepancy >6 months flags the record for P8 review.

•        Stat impossibility filters: Automated flags for statistically implausible entries (e.g. 350+ batting average over 10+ matches, bowling economy below 2.0 in T20s). System quarantines the record and sends to P8 review queue.

•        CricHeroes conflict detection: If self-reported stats deviate >15% from CricHeroes data for the same match/tournament, trigger conflict resolution flow. Player is notified and must resolve the discrepancy.

•        Duplicate account detection: Phone number + Aadhaar hash matching to prevent multiple accounts for the same player.

•        Fake academy certificates: Cross-reference submitted academy certificates against a curated database of known academies. Unknown academies are flagged — the coach confirmation from a non-registered academy cannot be used as a Tier 1 signal.

•        Community reporting: Allow verified coaches and selectors (P4/P5) to flag suspicious profiles. Three independent flags from verified P4/P5 accounts triggers automatic suspension pending P8 review.

•        SLA: P8 must resolve all fraud flags within 72 hours. High-impact tier changes (Tier 2 to Tier 1) require dual admin sign-off.

  

# 6. Player Progression Pathways

## 6.1 Canonical Pathways

SportX should support four primary progression pathways. Each has different data requirements and different SportX touchpoints.

### Pathway A: School → Academy → District → State

The most common pathway for players who start cricket young (before age 14). Most current domestic cricketers followed this route.

•        School stage (Age 8–14): Inter-house → inter-school → district school games (SGFI). SportX role: capture SGFI data, create pre-profile for school-level stars to give them a head start when they enter the formal pathway.

•        Academy stage (Age 12–18): Enrolment in registered academy, internal trials, city leagues. SportX role: academy admin (P5) onboards the player; profile created with coaching history and match records.

•        District stage (Age 16–22): DCA registration, official district matches. SportX role: core activation. Coach confirmation, CricHeroes sync, scorecard uploads, SportX Score calculation. Profile becomes scout-visible.

•        State stage (Age 18–25): State trials, age-group selection, senior domestic cricket. SportX role: auto-verification upgrade, benchmark comparison against state squad averages, analytics dashboard for scouts.

### Pathway B: Academy → District → State (Skipping Formal School)

Common for players who were identified early by academies but did not play formal school tournaments, or who come from rural areas where school cricket infrastructure is weak.

•        Academy as the origin point: No school data. Verification relies entirely on academy registration, coach history, and match records at club/inter-academy level.

•        Critical gap: These players often lack age-verified documentation. Aadhaar becomes the primary age anchor. If Aadhaar age does not match DCA registration age, conflict resolution is required.

•        SportX role: P5 Academy Admin is the trust anchor. Without academy admin verification, these players can only reach Tier 2.

### Pathway C: District → State → Higher Levels (Late Identifiers)

Players who were not identified at school or academy level but emerged through open district competitions. Often older (19–24) when they first come to attention.

•        Data typically exists on CricHeroes for these players. Verification is relatively straightforward.

•        The benchmarking problem is most acute here: these players have no coaches to advocate for them and no academy backing. The SportX Score is their only objective comparison tool.

•        Scout-facing profile is the critical deliverable for this pathway. A compelling, verified, data-rich profile is the difference between being invisible and getting a trial invitation.

### Pathway D: Direct to District (Talent Hunt / Open Trial Route)

Players discovered through talent hunts, open nets sessions, or viral video — who have no formal previous cricket documentation at all.

•        These are the hardest cases to verify. The platform must rely on intensive P4/P5 confirmation, video identity verification, and a longer Tier 2 probationary period before Tier 1.

•        SportX should not create a direct pipeline for this pathway at MVP — it creates too much verification overhead. The Opportunity Marketplace (scouts posting open trials) is the right mechanism for this player type at MVP.

# 7. How Scouts, Coaches & Selectors Currently Discover Players

## 7.1 IPL Franchise Scouting (Level 2 → Level 1)

IPL franchises represent the most sophisticated scouting operations in Indian cricket. Understanding their methodology informs SportX's product design — but also confirms that this level is largely served.

•        Scale: Franchises employ between 10 scouts (Rajasthan Royals' data analyst team) and 'as many as needed' (Mumbai Indians). KKR has approximately 25–30 scouts.

•        Process: Watch all televised BCCI domestic matches (Ranji, SMAT, VHT). Compile video libraries. Run data analysis on ball-by-ball statistics. Call shortlisted players to camps. Make auction recommendations.

•        Tools: CricViz for ball-by-ball analytics, internal databases, video footage from BCCI's taping program, Catapult for elite players' physical data.

•        The key insight from John Wright (ex-India coach, MI scout): 'With young players, numbers can tell a little bit, but not everything.' Qualitative assessment — composure, movement quality, adaptability — remains central.

•        Where it breaks down: 'Every talent across official Indian cricket is known to every franchise' — but this only applies to players who have already made it to SMAT or Ranji level. Below that, franchises are flying blind.

## 7.2 State-Level Selection (Level 3 → Level 2)

•        State selectors are typically ex-cricketers or BCCI-certified coaches serving as volunteers on state selection committees.

•        Primary source: DCA recommendations. Each DCA nominates players for state trials. The quality of nomination depends entirely on the DCA secretary's relationship with selectors — not on objective performance data.

•        WhatsApp scouting is the norm: Coaches forward video clips, scorecards, and recommendations via WhatsApp groups to selectors they have personal relationships with.

•        Open trials: Some progressive states (Maharashtra, Karnataka, Delhi) run open trials where any registered player can attend. Most states do not. Trial dates are announced informally with little advance notice.

•        Critical breakdown: The system structurally favours players from well-connected academies, urban centres, and states with historically strong cricket cultures (Mumbai, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu). Bihar, Assam, Jharkhand, and the North East are systemically underrepresented.

## 7.3 Academy and Grassroots Scouting

•        Academy scouting is the most personalised and informal system. A coach 'taking a player under their wing' — like Sudhindra Shinde with K.C. Cariappa — remains the primary discovery mechanism below district level.

•        Viral video discovery: One IPL scouting story involved a player being picked based entirely on a YouTube video. Scouts explicitly stated they tap their network for video recommendations. This confirms video is important but it lacks identity verification.

•        The talent hunt model (Rajasthan Royals, BCCI Talent Resource Development Wing): Organised events that go to specific geographies to conduct open trials. Important but infrequent — not a scalable ongoing mechanism.

## 7.4 Where the Existing Process Breaks Down — Summary

|**Breakdown Point**|**Root Cause**|**SportX Solution**|
|---|---|---|
|District players invisible to state selectors|No unified database. No digital scorecards in one place.|Verified player profiles with searchable stats, role-based filters, location filters|
|Selection based on connections not merit|WhatsApp recommendations = social network problem|Objective SportX Score as a merit index, visible to all verified scouts|
|Video exists but not verified|WhatsApp/YouTube video not linked to player identity|Video submission linked to verified profile; coach confirms player identity in video|
|No benchmark for players|No public comparison tool for sub-domestic level|IPL Benchmark Match feature: 'You are at 68% of IPL benchmark for your role'|
|Open trial announcements reach limited players|Announced through informal networks only|Opportunity Marketplace: scouts post trials, all Tier 1 players in relevant geography notified|
|Age fraud undermines credibility|Manual age checks, inconsistent DCA practices|Aadhaar-based age verification + cross-check with CricHeroes and SGFI data|
|Data lost across career stages|No portable performance record|Persistent player profile from academy through district through state|

  

# 8. Player Profile Data Points

## 8.1 Minimum Viable Profile (Required for Tier 2)

•        Identity: Full name, date of birth, Aadhaar number (hashed), profile photo, phone number, email

•        Geography: Home district, home state, nearest city

•        Playing credentials: Primary role (batter/bowler/WK/all-rounder), batting hand, bowling arm and type

•        Current status: Active playing level (Academy/District/State), current team/club, academy affiliation

•        Performance stats: For batters — innings, runs, average, strike rate, highest score, phase-split if available. For bowlers — overs, wickets, economy, average, strike rate, best figures.

•        Tournament history: At minimum 3 official matches with scorecards uploaded or CricHeroes sync

•        Fitness self-rating (NCA-BCCI scale 1–5)

•        Verification status: CricHeroes profile link, coach reference (P4 contact)

## 8.2 Enhanced Profile (Tier 1 — Scout-Visible)

•        Video highlights: Minimum 2 clips (batting/bowling) uploaded by player, identity confirmed by registered coach

•        Achievement history: Awards, best season performances, notable tournament results with context (opposition level, match format, pitch type)

•        Physical data: Height, weight, pace (for fast bowlers — self-reported with video evidence)

•        Qualitative self-assessment: Pressure handling rating (1–5), mental toughness rating (1–5) — both acknowledged as self-reported on profile

•        Academy/coach history: Academy names, duration, coach names with verifiable contact

•        External profile links: CricHeroes URL, YouTube highlights, Instagram handle

•        Phase-split statistics: Powerplay, middle overs, death overs performance (where CricHeroes data permits)

## 8.3 Data Points That Build Credibility (Scout Priorities)

Based on research into what IPL scouts and state selectors actually value:

|**Data Point**|**Scout Value**|**Why It Matters**|**Source**|
|---|---|---|---|
|Consistency across 2+ seasons|Very High|One-season outliers are discounted|Career match history|
|Performance in knockout/high-stakes matches|Very High|Pressure composure is unobservable from averages alone|Tournament stage metadata|
|Video of skill execution (not just highlights)|High|Scouts assess technique, not just results|Player upload + coach verification|
|Opposition quality context|High|A 50 vs a bottom-tier district team ≠ a 50 vs a state-level academy side|Tournament metadata|
|Coach confirmation with contact|High|A named, reachable coach is the strongest trust signal below DCA|P4 verification|
|Age-verified documentation|High|Age fraud is rampant — verified age is a premium signal|Aadhaar cross-check|
|Phase-split bowling economy|Medium-High|Powerplay vs death overs economy tells very different stories|CricHeroes / scorecard|
|Fitness self-rating|Medium|Scouts screen out physically unfit candidates early|Self-reported + video confirmation|
|Batting strike rate in powerplay vs death|Medium-High|Role-specific IPL selection criterion|CricHeroes / scorecard|

  

# 9. Value SportX Provides to Each Stakeholder

## 9.1 Players

•        Visibility without connections: A verified, data-rich profile that is discoverable by state selectors, academy scouts, and franchise analysts regardless of which city the player is from or which coach they know.

•        Benchmarking: SportX Score and IPL Benchmark Match give a player objective, data-driven feedback on where they stand relative to professional standards. Currently, players have no way to know this.

•        Portable career record: A persistent, verified career history that travels with the player across academies, clubs, and districts — not locked in any single institution's records.

•        Direct access to trials: Opportunity Marketplace gives players direct access to trial invitations from academies, clubs, and scouts — bypassing the WhatsApp network.

•        Motivational feedback loop: Profile completion percentage, tier progression, and score improvement create a game-like progression mechanic that keeps engagement high.

## 9.2 Coaches

•        Portfolio showcase: Coaches can demonstrate their players' achievements and progression through an objective, verified platform — a professional credibility tool for coaches who rely on player outcomes for their reputation.

•        Batch management: P4 coaches can manage multiple player profiles, confirm their data, and track their progression in one place — replacing the WhatsApp-and-Excel workflow.

•        Scout connection: Verified coaches on the platform become a trusted node in the discovery network. Scouts who trust a coach's confirmations will return to that coach's recommended players.

•        Revenue from confirmed players: If a player from their roster gets a trial or selection through SportX, the coach gains platform credibility that drives new student enrolment.

## 9.3 Academies

•        Digital roster management: P5 Academy Admin can onboard their entire player roster to the platform, replacing paper registers with verified digital profiles.

•        Institutional credibility: Academies that produce players who progress through the SportX Tier system build a verifiable track record of player development — a marketing asset for student acquisition.

•        Batch verification authority: Academies gain the highest trust-granting power on the platform (immediate +7 verification point boost on enrollment confirmation) — incentivising early adoption.

•        Talent retention: Players who see their SportX Score improve and gain scout visibility are more likely to continue at the academy long-term.

## 9.4 Scouts & Selectors

•        Replacement for WhatsApp scouting: A structured, searchable database of verified player profiles with standardised statistics — filters by role, age, district, batting style, bowling type, SportX Score, and IPL readiness.

•        Remote talent sourcing: A scout sitting in Mumbai can search for 'left-arm fast bowlers under 21 from Bihar with economy under 7.0' and get verified results within seconds. Currently impossible.

•        Trust infrastructure: Every profile has a verification tier badge and an auditable trust score. Scouts know exactly how reliable each data point is before they decide to invite a player for a trial.

•        Frictionless trial invitation: One-click trial invitation from the scout dashboard, delivered directly to the player. No intermediary, no club politics.

•        Women's cricket pipeline: A dedicated filter for female players allows women's cricket scouts to access the most data-poor segment of the talent pool — a currently near-zero visibility layer.

## 9.5 Parents

•        Informed investment: Parents spending ₹1–2 lakh per year on cricket coaching gain an objective, data-driven assessment of whether their child is on track for a professional career — reducing emotional and financial misdirection.

•        Safety for minors: All scout communications are routed through the parent inbox for players under 18. No direct contact between scouts and minor players outside the verified platform.

•        Transition control: Parent account automatically transitions to full player control on the player's 18th birthday (automated cron job).

•        Visibility without scam exposure: Currently, parents are targeted by fraudulent 'India trials' on Instagram. The SportX Opportunity Marketplace is a verified alternative — only certified scouts and academies can post trial invitations.

## 10.2 SportX's Structural Differentiation

The core differentiation is not features — it is the combination of three things that no existing platform provides together:

1.     Trust architecture: An independently verified player profile with multi-persona confirmation, OCR scorecards, and Aadhaar-based age verification. CricHeroes has the data but not the trust layer. CricViz has the trust but not the data at grassroots level.

2.     Scout-side B2B product: A purpose-built discovery dashboard for state selectors, academy scouts, and pathway coaches — with role-based filters, SportX Score ranking, and trial invitation workflow. No platform in the market has built this for the Level 4 population.

3.     Longitudinal career record: A portable player profile that captures the full progression from Academy → District → State. This longitudinal data becomes a unique asset over time — SportX will have tracked players before anyone else knew their names.

## 10.3 The CricHeroes Relationship

CricHeroes is simultaneously the most important data partner and the most significant competitive risk. The strategic resolution is clear:

•        CricHeroes is a community and scoring product. Their user is the local cricketer who wants to track his match stats and share them with friends. Their business model is ad-supported.

•        SportX is a talent discovery and verification product. Our user is the state selector who needs to find the best U-19 fast bowler in Assam. Our business model is B2B subscription.

•        These are compatible, not competitive, as long as SportX does not try to replicate CricHeroes' scoring app. The right move is to build on top of CricHeroes data, not around it.

•        Partnership priority: Pursue a CricHeroes data integration as a top-3 business development priority in the first 6 months. The alternative — building a competing scoring app — is a 2-year detour that plays into CricHeroes' core strength.

  

# 11. Strategic Recommendations for SportX

## 11.1 MVP Scope (Based on Research)

The research confirms the Level 4 → Level 3 thesis. The MVP should be scoped around a single proof-of-concept transaction:

**_One district-level player gets shortlisted for a state trial through SportX — without a personal connection to the selector._**

Every product decision should be evaluated against this test.

## 11.2 Go-to-Market Recommendation

•        Launch in one state only: Rajasthan, Bihar, or Assam recommended. These states have acute visibility gaps, large district player populations, active CricHeroes communities, and state associations that are known to be reform-minded. Avoid Maharashtra and Karnataka for the MVP — they are too well-served by existing networks.

•        Activate P5 Academy Admins first: Sign up the top 20–30 academies in the launch state before public launch. Their player rosters provide the initial density needed for scouts to find value on day one.

•        Sign one state selector: A single verified state-level selector (P6) using the platform in the first 3 months is the highest-value proof point. One trial invitation generated through the platform is the MVP success metric.

•        CricHeroes partnership as launch infrastructure: Negotiate data access before or at launch. The alternative (asking players to manually enter stats) creates friction that will kill activation rates.

## 11.3 Women's Cricket — Do Not Ignore

All existing documents in the SportX folder have entirely omitted women's cricket. This is a strategic oversight. Key facts:

•        BCCI has invested heavily in women's cricket since 2018 — Women's IPL (WPL) launched in 2023, with growing franchise spending on scouting.

•        Women's talent at district level is arguably even more invisible than men's — the existing scouting infrastructure for women's cricket below state level is almost non-existent.

•        WPL franchise scouts are actively looking for discovery tools for women's cricket specifically. This is an underserved B2B buyer that SportX can own.

•        Recommendation: Build the product as gender-neutral from day one. Separate female player profiles and female scout filters from the outset — do not bolt women's cricket on as an afterthought post-launch.
