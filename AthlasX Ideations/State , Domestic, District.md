# 2. Are Domestic Players More Recognised Than State Players?

This is one of the most misunderstood distinctions in Indian cricket. The short answer: Yes — domestic (L2) players are significantly more recognised than state-level age group (L3) players, and the gap is structural, not just about performance.

## 2.1 Clarifying the Terminology

_In Indian cricket, 'State Level' informally refers to both age-group representation (Vijay Merchant, Cooch Behar, CK Nayudu) AND senior domestic cricket (Ranji, SMAT, VHT). This ambiguity causes confusion. AthlasX must define these precisely._

|**Term Used**|**What It Actually Means**|**AthlasX Classification**|
|---|---|---|
|'State Level' (common usage)|Any cricket played under a state association — could mean U-16 or Ranji|L3 (age group) or L2 (senior domestic) — must be specified|
|'Domestic Cricket'|Senior BCCI tournaments: Ranji, SMAT, Vijay Hazare, Duleep|L2 — Senior Domestic|
|'State Age Group'|U-16, U-19, U-23 tournaments under state association|L3 — National Pathway|
|'First Class Cricket'|Ranji Trophy, Duleep Trophy — 4-day, red-ball cricket|L2 top tier|
|'List A Cricket'|Vijay Hazare Trophy — 50-over format|L2 mid tier|
|'T20 Domestic'|Syed Mushtaq Ali Trophy|L2 mid tier — most IPL-visible|

## 2.2 Why Domestic (L2) Players Are More Recognised

•        Media coverage: Ranji Trophy, SMAT, and VHT matches are covered by ESPNcricinfo, Cricbuzz, and increasingly by JioHotstar. Ball-by-ball stats are publicly available. IPL scouts don't need to travel — the data comes to them.

•        BCCI registration with ID: Every senior domestic player has a BCCI Player ID. Their records are centrally stored and searchable. An L3 (age group) player's record is only in the state association's files.

•        IPL visibility: SMAT is the primary hunting ground for IPL franchises. Every SMAT performance is watched. CK Nayudu Trophy performance is watched by fewer than 5% of the scouting community.

•        Financial incentives signal seriousness: A Ranji player earning ₹40,000–₹60,000 per day is a professional cricketer. An L3 U-19 player earning ₹20,000 per match is still a student. Scouts treat them differently.

•        The Duleep Trophy distinction: The Duleep Trophy (5 zonal teams, senior players) is the single most-watched domestic tournament by selectors — it is explicitly a national team preview event. No age-group tournament has this profile.

## 2.3 The Cruel Gap: Outstanding L3 Players Who Are Invisible

Here is the structural injustice AthlasX exists to fix. Consider two real scenarios:

_Scenario A: A player averages 62 in the Cooch Behar Trophy (U-19) over two seasons for Bihar. He is the top scorer in his state. No national media covers Bihar's matches. No selector from outside Bihar was present. He gets no U-19 national camp invitation. He ages out at 19 and moves to CK Nayudu — where the same thing happens. By 23 he has never been seen._

_Scenario B: A player averages 38 in the SMAT for a major state (Maharashtra). His stats are on ESPNcricinfo. Three IPL franchises have him flagged in their database. He gets an IPL call-up despite lower absolute numbers, simply because his data is visible and trusted._

The recognition gap is not about talent — it is about data visibility, geographic bias, and media coverage. This is exactly AthlasX's problem to solve at L3.

# 4. What Scouts & Selectors Actually Look For

The gap between stats and selection is where most talented players get lost. Scouts and selectors evaluate a player across five distinct dimensions — stats are only one of them.

## 4.1 The Five Dimensions of Selection

### Dimension 1: Statistical Performance (the threshold)

Statistics are the entry gate — they tell selectors who to watch, not who to select. No selector will watch a player without basic numbers that justify attention. But stats alone have never made a player.

•        Batting: Average, strike rate, phase-split (powerplay/middle/death), performance in knockout matches vs group stage, performance vs quality opposition.

•        Bowling: Average, economy, strike rate, wickets in pressure overs (pp overs 1-6, death overs 17-20), performance in final innings, performance on challenging pitches.

•        The consistency test: A player with one outstanding season is interesting. A player with two or three consistent seasons is valuable. Selectors explicitly discount single-season performances.

### Dimension 2: Technique & Skill Quality (what scouts physically watch)

This is what scouts travel to see and what videos are requested for. Numbers cannot capture this — only observation can.

•        Batters: Footwork against spin (placement of front foot, use of crease), head position at impact, shoulder alignment, shot selection under pressure, ability to play both sides of the wicket, managing swing and seam in first 10 overs.

•        Pace bowlers: Run-up rhythm and repeatability, wrist position at release, ability to generate pace from a shorter run-up (IPL requirement), seam position, reverse swing skills, yorker execution under pressure.

•        Spinners: Flight and dip variation, disguised change of pace, ability to take wickets on flat pitches (not just on turning tracks), mental fortitude when hit.

•        John Wright (ex-India coach, MI scout): 'With young players, numbers can tell a little bit, but not everything.' This is the definitive statement of how scouts think.

### Dimension 3: Temperament & Pressure Performance

Arguably the most important dimension and the hardest to quantify. Selectors specifically look for:

•        Performance in knockout matches vs group stage. A player who scores in finals and semi-finals is valued exponentially more than one who scores in low-stakes group games.

•        Behaviour after dismissal or when hit for boundaries. Does the player regroup, or do they lose their method? Scouts watch the 5 overs after a setback more carefully than the 5 good overs.

•        Score in a chase vs score batting first. Chasing under pressure is a specific skill — selectors treat it separately.

•        Body language in the field when the team is under pressure. Selectors watch fielding intensity as a proxy for mental engagement.

_For AthlasX: Knockout match performance should be a separate, highlighted metric on every profile. A player's average in knockout stages vs group stages should be visible to scouts with a clear label. This is currently available nowhere for L3 and L4 players._

### Dimension 4: Role-Specific Fit

Teams don't select good cricketers — they select cricketers who fill specific roles. This is one of the most underappreciated aspects of selection.

•        IPL franchises select by role: 'left-arm pacer who can bowl the powerplay' or 'explosive opener who can target off-spin'. A right-handed batter with a 45 average who struggles against left-arm swing is less valuable than a 35-average batter who dominates left-arm swing.

•        State selectors select for team balance: If the state team already has four right-handed top-order batters, a left-handed batter with a slightly lower average gets preferential consideration.

•        Age-group selectors select for developmental trajectory: A 16-year-old with a 32 average who has excellent footwork against spin is prioritised over an 18-year-old with a 40 average who has technical flaws.

_For AthlasX: Role-specific profile labels (powerplay specialist, death bowler, anchor, floater, etc.) should be explicitly defined on player profiles. Scouts should be able to filter by role, not just by stats._

### Dimension 5: Physical Fitness & Athleticism

•        Fitness is a screening criterion — players below the NCA fitness threshold are eliminated before technical assessment begins.

•        Pace bowlers: Minimum pace thresholds. At L3→L2, 125+ km/h is broadly expected for pace bowlers. At L2→IPL, 130+ km/h is the informal threshold. Below these, a bowler needs exceptional accuracy and variation to compensate.

•        NCA fitness benchmarks (from AthlasX research doc): 2km run under 8:30 (men), standing broad jump 2.3m+, 30m sprint under 4.0 seconds. These are the baseline filters used at BCCI age-group camps.

•        Injury history: A player with repeated injury flags is deprioritised regardless of stats. Selectors at every level explicitly factor durability into selection decisions.

## 4.2 The Scout's Physical Observation Checklist

When a scout physically attends a match to watch a specific player, here is what they document:

|**Observation Area**|**What They Watch**|**Red Flags**|**Green Flags**|
|---|---|---|---|
|Pre-match warm-up|Energy level, movement quality, interaction with teammates|Disengaged, stiff movement, isolated|High energy, athletic warm-up, leader presence|
|First 10 balls (batter)|Initial movement, foot positioning, early shot selection|Hard hands, no foot movement, edging outside off|Soft hands, positive footwork, leaving well|
|Under pressure (batter)|Response after dot balls, after a false shot, when crowd reacts|Tension in body, playing across the line, losing patience|Reset after bad ball, stick to method, clear mind|
|Dismissal reaction|Body language when walking off|Aggressive reaction, helmet throw, blaming conditions|Calm acceptance, professional, head up|
|First over (bowler)|Run-up rhythm, release point consistency, seam position|Front-on action, jerky run-up, no seam presentation|High action, smooth acceleration, upright seam|
|When hit for boundaries|Response to being attacked — next delivery|Short-pitched reactive bowling, loss of length|Disciplined return to plan, variation in response|
|Fielding|Effort, dive, throw accuracy, ground fielding|Jog rather than sprint, avoiding hard chances|Full effort, clean stops, accurate arm|
|Between overs / down time|How player behaves when not in action|Disconnected, on phone, chatting informally|Watching the game, talking to captain, alert|

## 4.3 The 'Invisible Factors' Selectors Weigh

Beyond the observable, selectors carry institutional biases and contextual weights that are almost never documented but are very real:

•        Academy pedigree: A player from MRF Pace Foundation, NCA, or a franchise-affiliated academy carries an implicit trust bonus. The assumption is that coaches at these academies have already filtered for talent and attitude.

•        Coach endorsement: A personal call from a respected ex-cricketer or coach is the most powerful non-statistical signal. 'Anil Bhai called me about this kid' opens doors that a 55 Ranji average cannot.

•        State association politics: In several states, selection committees have established relationships with certain academies or clubs. Players from outside these networks need to outperform the competition significantly to break in.

•        Social media presence: An increasingly relevant signal at IPL scout level. A player with viral videos demonstrating skill creates awareness in the scouting community that would otherwise take months to build.

_For AthlasX: The platform cannot eliminate political bias — but it can make merit-based evidence so clear and accessible that overlooking it becomes harder to justify. A verified 55-average player whose profile is on the scout's dashboard is harder to ignore than an identical player who exists only on paper._

  

# 5. How Unrecognised Players Survive in the Cricket Ecosystem

This is the question that defines AthlasX's emotional and social purpose. The answer is a complex, often heartbreaking mix of financial strategy, career pivots, and quiet persistence.

## 5.1 The Financial Reality by Level

|**Player Type**|**Annual Income from Cricket**|**Gap Fillers**|**Career Horizon**|
|---|---|---|---|
|L4 District (no BCCI pay)|₹0 from cricket officially|Part-time jobs, academy coaching, private tuitions, family support|Usually exits cricket by age 22–24 if not progressing|
|L3 U-16 State|~₹2L/season (BCCI)|Family support is the primary financial backbone|Parents carrying the cost; player has no independence|
|L3 U-19 State|~₹3–4L/season (BCCI)|Academy stipends, local tournament prize money, coaching junior players|Sustainable short-term; crisis hits at age 19 if no U-23 call|
|L3 U-23 State|~₹4–5L/season (BCCI)|Private league cricket (TNPL, KPL, local T20 leagues), coaching income|Last window — must break into senior domestic by 23 or pivot|
|L2 Senior Domestic (new)|₹17–22L/season (BCCI)|Self-sustaining for the first time; some also in IPL auction|Established professional — 3–5 year career window|
|L2 Senior (Mumbai)|₹35–50L/season (BCCI + MCA)|Endorsements possible; IPL base price ₹20L+|Full professional career; financial security|

## 5.2 The Five Survival Strategies

### Strategy 1: Government / PSU / Railways Jobs

The most common survival strategy for serious cricketers who reach L3 but don't break into senior domestic. Government jobs with sports quota include Railways, State Police, State PWD, Banks (SBI, Bank of Baroda), and Army/Navy/Air Force.

•        These jobs allow cricketers to continue playing — Railways, for instance, has its own cricket team that competes in Ranji Trophy.

•        The trade-off: Government job security at the cost of training intensity. A player working 8-hour shifts cannot train like a professional.

•        Famous examples: Many state-level cricketers you've never heard of are state police officers, bank clerks, or railway employees — playing weekend cricket while holding full-time jobs.

### Strategy 2: Academy Coaching Income

The most cricket-adjacent survival path. Players who don't make the senior domestic squad often transition into coaching at private academies — sometimes as early as age 21.

•        Income range: ₹15,000–₹50,000/month depending on academy reputation and city.

•        Double role: Many player-coaches continue playing district cricket while coaching — using their morning slots for personal training and afternoons for coaching batches.

•        The irony: These coaches are often the best talent identifiers at district level. Their knowledge of technical fundamentals is high. AthlasX's P4 coach persona is largely built around this population.

### Strategy 3: Private League Cricket

The explosion of BCCI-affiliated and semi-official T20 leagues has created a meaningful income stream for players stuck between L3 and L2.

•        State T20 leagues: TNPL (Tamil Nadu), KPL (Karnataka Premier League), MPL (Madhya Pradesh Premier League), and equivalent leagues in 15+ states now pay ₹50,000–₹3,00,000 per contract per season.

•        These leagues are increasingly watched by IPL scouts. They are the bridge between L3 obscurity and L2 visibility that AthlasX should integrate with.

•        Private leagues (outside BCCI): Club T20 leagues, corporate tournaments, invitational events — pay ₹5,000–₹25,000 per match. Not scouted, but provide income and match sharpness.

### Strategy 4: Trial Tourism

A deeply problematic but widespread survival behaviour. Players travel to multiple states for open trials, paying ₹2,000–₹10,000 per trial in the hope of getting noticed.

•        Players from Bihar or Jharkhand often travel to Hyderabad, Pune, or Delhi for trials — spending ₹15,000–₹40,000 on travel, accommodation, and trial fees for each trip.

•        The scam industry: Instagram and YouTube are flooded with fraudulent 'India trial' advertisements targeting desperate players and their families. Players have lost ₹50,000–₹2,00,000 to these scams.

•        AthlasX's role here is critical: The Opportunity Marketplace allows verified scouts to post legitimate trial invitations. A verified trial listing with a named, verified organiser is the direct antidote to trial scams.

### Strategy 5: Quiet Exit

The most common outcome for the majority of serious district and state-level cricketers. By age 22–25, most players who haven't broken into senior domestic cricket make a rational decision to prioritise career and family over cricket.

•        They don't 'quit' — they simply reduce their commitment. A player who was training 6 days a week at 18 is training once a week at 24, playing weekend club matches while building a non-cricket career.

•        The emotional cost: Interviews with ex-district cricketers consistently reveal regret not about the decision to exit, but about never knowing if they were good enough — because they were never given a fair, objective assessment.

_This is AthlasX's deepest purpose. Not every player it surfaces will go on to play Ranji. But every player who has a verified, benchmarked profile knows exactly where they stand relative to the standard they're trying to reach. That honesty — delivered with data — is itself a form of respect that the current system withholds._

## 5.3 The Parents' Perspective — The Hidden Cost

The financial survival story cannot be told without the family dimension. Most district and state-level cricketers in India are partially or fully family-funded until they reach senior domestic level.

•        Average family investment per serious cricketer: ₹15,000–₹2,00,000+ per year on coaching fees, kit, travel, tournament entry fees, and academy fees.

•        Peak investment age: 14–19. This is when academy fees are highest, travel for trials is most frequent, and BCCI pay doesn't yet exist.

•        The ROI conversation: Most families eventually force a 'cricket or career' decision around age 20–22 when no professional income has materialised. Players who haven't received a state trial invitation by this age face enormous family pressure to exit.

•        AthlasX's value to parents: An objective, verified benchmark that tells a parent 'your son is at 68% of the L3 minimum standard — here is what he needs to improve' is more valuable than 5 years of coach opinions, because it is honest and actionable.

•        The trial scam protection: A verified Opportunity Marketplace specifically protects parents from spending ₹50,000+ on fraudulent trial 'opportunities'. This is a concrete, measurable safety value AthlasX provides.
