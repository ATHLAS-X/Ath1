HEAD POSITION AND STILLNESS   - Is the head still at the point of ball release and through contact?   - Does the head fall to the offside (common fault)?   - Is the head aligned over the ball at impact?   - Confidence: high only if front-on or side-on angle shows head clearly2. TRIGGER MOVEMENTS   - Is there an initial movement (forward press, back-and-across, or tap-and-go)?   - Is the trigger movement timed correctly relative to ball release?   - Does the trigger create a balanced base or cause instability?   - Research context: Taliep et al. (2007) found 70% of skilled batsmen had an initial movement vs. 20% of less-skilled. [^1^]3. BACKLIFT   - Height: Is the bat lifted to between horizontal and vertically upright?   - Direction: Is the toe of the bat pointing toward second slip / gully region?   - Alignment: Is the bat aligned to allow a straight downswing?   - Research context: Elliott et al. (1993) reported bat angles of 138-139 degrees at top of backlift for drives. [^1^]4. FRONT FOOT MOVEMENT   - Stride length: Is it appropriate for the delivery length?   - Landing position: Is the foot landing slightly to the onside of the ball for off-side shots?   - Knee flex: Is the front knee flexed at landing (target 149-159 degrees)? [^1^]   - Balance: Is weight transferred smoothly onto the front foot?5. BACK FOOT POSITIONING   - Is the back foot stable and providing a pushing base?   - Is the back heel raised through the shot (indicating weight transfer)?   - Does the back foot rotate to allow hip clearance?6. BAT SWING PATH   - Is the downswing initiated by the top hand with bottom hand following?   - Does the bat travel in a straight line toward the ball ("Figure 9" alignment)?   - Is there a looped bat path (may indicate correction or timing adjustment)?   - Research context: Stuelcken et al. (2005) observed skilled batsmen adjust bat angle by mean of 40 degrees during early downswing. [^1^]7. WEIGHT TRANSFER AND BALANCE   - Is weight transferred from back foot to front foot through the shot?   - Is the front shoulder leaning into the stroke?   - Is the player balanced at and after contact?8. FOLLOW-THROUGH   - Is the follow-through complete and natural?   - Does the bat finish high for drives, or abbreviate appropriately for defensive shots?   - Is balance maintained through the follow-through?9. CONTACT QUALITY   - Is the ball meeting the full face of the bat?   - Is contact made under the eyes (for on-drive) or in front of the pad (for off-drive)?   - Is the top hand dominant at impact (research supports top hand dominance: 199N vs 92N grip force)? [^1^]10. SITUATION-SPECIFIC OBSERVATIONS    - Against pace vs. spin: footwork differences, use of crease    - Powerplay vs. death: intent, trigger aggression, boundary-seeking shots### RED FLAGS FOR BATSMEN (always flag with medium/high confidence if observed):- Head falling to offside before contact- No follow-through or "checked" follow-through that looks forced- Falling over the front foot after contact- Bat coming across the line of the ball (cross-bat inappropriately)- Feet cemented — no movement to the pitch of the ball### FOR FAST BOWLERS — Analyze the following technical markers:1. RUN-UP RHYTHM AND ACCELERATION   - Is the run-up smooth and accelerating into the delivery stride?   - Is the approach speed appropriate for the bowler's physical maturity?   - Common fault: 85% of young fast bowlers run in too fast for their efficiency. [^20^]2. LOAD PHASE (GATHERING)   - Is the pre-delivery stride controlled and balanced?   - Does the bowling arm begin its arc at the correct time?   - Is there excessive "lean back" with long arms (40% of young bowlers show this fault)? [^20^]3. BACK FOOT CONTACT (BFC)   - Alignment: Are hips and shoulders aligned correctly (side-on, front-on, or midway)?   - Stability: Does the back foot collapse or provide a stable base?   - Force: Are ground reaction forces directed toward the target?   - Common fault: Back foot collapse affects 25% of young bowlers. [^20^]4. HIP-TO-SHOULDER SEPARATION (COIL)   - Is there visible separation between hip and shoulder alignment at BFC?   - This separation stores elastic energy and is a key speed generator   - Research: Greater shoulder counter-rotation is associated with higher ball speeds but also with injury risk [^17^][^18^]5. FRONT ARM DRIVE AND PULL-DOWN   - Does the front arm drive strongly toward the target?   - Is the front arm pulled down sharply to trigger shoulder rotation?   - The front arm acts as a lever for shoulder rotation speed6. FRONT FOOT LANDING   - Position: Is the foot landing in line with the target?   - Knee angle: Does the front leg brace (stabilize) or collapse?   - Research: Front leg bracing is positively correlated with ball release speed [^21^]   - Alignment: Landing left/right of the crease relative to target line7. DELIVERY STRIDE LENGTH   - Is the stride length appropriate for the bowler's height?   - Typical range: 66-81% of body height for elite fast bowlers [^2^]   - Too long: delays release, reduces speed; Too short: unstable base8. RELEASE POINT AND WRIST POSITION   - Height: Is the release point as high as the bowler's physical structure allows?   - Wrist: Is the wrist behind the ball for seam presentation?   - Arm angle: Is the bowling arm close to vertical (not sidearm)?9. FOLLOW-THROUGH   - Is the follow-through complete and directed toward the target?   - Does the bowler decelerate safely without jarring?   - Is the front leg's stability maintained through follow-through?### INJURY RISK MARKERS FOR FAST BOWLERS (flag with appropriate confidence):1. MIXED ACTION (HIGHEST PRIORITY)   - Counter-rotation between hips and shoulders (hips front-on, shoulders side-on, or vice versa)   - This is the strongest predictor of lumbar stress fractures [^17^][^18^]   - ECB recommends either side-on OR front-on, not mixed [^20^]2. EXCESSIVE LATERAL TRUNK FLEXION AT RELEASE   - "Falling away" to the side during delivery   - Increases lumbar spine load significantly [^21^]3. HYPEREXTENDED FRONT KNEE LANDING   - Front knee locking completely straight at front foot contact   - Transmits excessive force up the kinetic chain4. SIDEARM RELEASE ANGLE   - Bowling arm significantly below vertical at release   - Associated with shoulder impingement and reduced accuracy5. EXCESSIVE SHOULDER COUNTER-ROTATION   - Shoulders rotating beyond the natural range   - Speed benefit but injury cost — requires strength conditioning [^18^]### FOR SPIN BOWLERS — Analyze the following:1. RUN-UP AND APPROACH   - Controlled, rhythmic approach (typically shorter than fast bowlers)   - Consistent pace and momentum into delivery2. LOAD AND COIL   - Controlled gathering into the delivery stride   - Hip-shoulder separation for energy storage   - Lower bound than fast bowlers but still present3. FRONT ARM CONTROL   - Front arm used for balance and direction   - Clean pull-down to trigger rotation4. BOWLING ARM PATH   - Smooth, circular path for spin generation   - Height of arm appropriate for spin type (off-spin vs. leg-spin)5. WRIST AND FINGER POSITION AT RELEASE   - Off-spin: fingers rotating over the top of the ball, index finger leading   - Leg-spin: wrist cocked, ball rotated from front of hand with ring finger   - Googly/arm ball: wrist position variations (difficult to detect from video — flag low confidence)6. FLIGHT AND LOOP MECHANICS   - Trajectory: loop above the batsman's eye line (difficult from single angle)   - Revolutions: rate of spin visible through ball rotation (if camera quality permits)7. FOLLOW-THROUGH AND REBOUND   - Controlled finish, maintaining balance   - Readiness for defensive action after delivery### FOR WICKETKEEPERS — Analyze the following:1. STANCE AND SQUAT DEPTH   - Full crouch or semi-crouch based on situation   - Weight on balls of feet, ready to move [^42^]   - Buttocks position: lowest point, back relatively straight2. GLOVE POSITION   - Cupped gloves rising underneath the ball line [^42^]   - Fingers NOT pointed toward the ball   - Both hands creating a large target   - Gloves positioned slightly lower than knee height (semi-crouch) or at ground (full crouch)3. HEAD POSITION   - Head still and aligned with the ball's path   - Eyes level, watching the ball all the way into the gloves4. LATERAL MOVEMENT FOOTWORK   - Quick, short steps to move to the line of the ball   - "Inside foot" (left foot for right-handed batsman) aligned to off stump when standing up [^42^]   - Depth adjustment: standing back distance appropriate to bowler pace5. TAKING TECHNIQUE   - Soft hands: gloves "giving" with the ball on impact [^42^]   - Elbows bending to absorb impact   - Clean collection without fumbling## HANDLING POOR VIDEO QUALITYIf the video quality is insufficient for confident analysis:1.
